# Evidence Check

## What to do

For each major claim in the content under review — especially claims about:

- ownership ("I led...", "I owned...")
- leadership or collaboration ("I aligned stakeholders...", "I worked with...")
- impact ("this improved...", "this reduced...")
- complexity ("this was a complex system...")
- scale ("40+ institutions...", "across six layouts...")
- business value
- systems thinking or strategic framing
- research conclusions
- seniority (anything implying a level of responsibility or judgment)

Classify it as one of:

- **Supported** — the content itself shows concrete evidence (a specific
  decision, a specific artifact, a specific mechanism) that backs the claim.
- **Partially supported** — some evidence exists but doesn't fully cover the
  claim's scope (e.g. ownership of one part of a multi-part claim).
- **Unsupported** — the claim is stated with no accompanying evidence at all.
- **Overstated** — evidence exists but doesn't support the strength or scope
  of the language used (e.g. a single decision described as "redesigning
  the entire system").

For each classification, ask: is the evidence shown sufficient for an
external reviewer who knows nothing about the project going in? Not "would
Jamie's own memory of the project fill this in" — the portfolio content has
to carry the weight itself.

## Output

For each claim you check, give: the claim, the classification, why, and one
of the following recommendations when the classification isn't "Supported":

- show stronger evidence (name what evidence would help)
- narrow the claim (state specifically how to narrow it)
- rewrite the claim (state specifically what more accurate language would say)
- remove the claim

## What not to do

- Do not default to charitable readings. If a claim could be read either as
  supported or overstated, say so and lean toward the more skeptical read —
  that's the point of this check.
- Do not invent evidence that isn't in the source material to make a claim
  "Supported."
- Prefer precise, plain language over impressive-sounding language when
  recommending a rewrite.
