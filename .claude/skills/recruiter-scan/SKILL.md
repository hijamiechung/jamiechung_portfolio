# Recruiter Scan

Simulate a recruiter who does not read every paragraph. Review in layers,
and do not let later, slower layers retroactively excuse what the earlier,
faster layers failed to convey.

## First 5 seconds

Can I tell:
- what kind of designer this is?
- what they're good at?
- whether I should keep reading?

If the honest answer to any of these is no, say so plainly — this is the
layer most likely to lose someone, and it's the one portfolios most often
overinvest in decoration instead of clarity for.

## First 30 seconds

Can I understand:
- project context?
- role?
- scale?
- ownership?
- the problem being solved?
- why this work matters?

## First 60 seconds

Can I explain this candidate to a hiring manager in one sentence? Write that
sentence as you'd actually say it. If you can't produce one, that's itself
the finding.

## Evaluate throughout

- clarity
- hierarchy
- scannability
- differentiation from other portfolios
- credibility
- signal-to-noise ratio
- role clarity
- project relevance to the roles being targeted
- seniority signal
- memorability

## What not to do

- Do not assume the recruiter will read case-study body text closely, or at
  all, on a first pass — evaluate what's visible without deep reading.
- Do not credit content that exists but is buried, small, or de-emphasized
  as if it were prominent.
- Do not soften the assessment because the underlying work is good — a
  scan failure is a communication failure independent of work quality, and
  should be reported as one.
