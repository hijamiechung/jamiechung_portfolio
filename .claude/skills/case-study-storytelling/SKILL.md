# Case Study Storytelling

## Structure

Evaluate (or draft) a case study against this structure. Every part should
be present and load-bearing — not just visitable in order.

### System

What ecosystem, actors, constraints, and dependencies existed before any
design work happened? A reader unfamiliar with the project should understand
the world it lived in before seeing a single screen.

### Tension

Where did the system's existing logic fail to match human, organizational,
or operational reality? This is the actual problem — not "the UI was
outdated" but a genuine mismatch between how the system worked and how
people needed it to work.

### Observation

What did the designer personally notice, question, interpret, connect, or
reframe? This is where individual judgment becomes visible. Flag any section
that hides this behind passive research-team language ("we conducted
research and found...") instead of naming what the designer specifically
saw.

### Design Decision

What structural decision followed from the observation — architecture,
workflow, hierarchy, a relationship model, an interaction model, a
prioritization call, a decision framework? This should visibly follow from
the Tension and Observation, not appear disconnected from them.

Do not let this section jump straight to visual polish. Visual decisions are
a consequence of structural ones, not a substitute for them.

### Outcome

What became possible afterward? Use real, specific evidence. If numeric
metrics are unavailable, concrete non-numeric outcomes are acceptable:
alignment reached, something implemented, a pattern reused elsewhere,
clearer ownership, reduced ambiguity, consistency achieved, a decision
enabled. Never fabricate or infer impact that isn't stated somewhere in the
source material.

## What to flag

- Any case study missing one of the five parts, or where a part exists only
  as a heading with no real content underneath.
- Sections that read as generic UX-process narration (discovery →
  wireframes → testing → ship) rather than showing actual reasoning.
- Outcomes stated with unwarranted precision or confidence when the
  underlying evidence is thin.
- Places where the visual/UI description substitutes for a missing
  structural decision.

## What not to do

- Do not invent an Observation, Design Decision, or Outcome that isn't
  supported by the source content — say the section is missing or weak
  instead of filling it in with plausible-sounding filler.
- Do not rewrite copy yourself unless explicitly asked; this skill's default
  output is evaluation, not a finished rewrite.
